[hey, psycho!]

I lay on the stretcher, taut. Blue-haired, blue-eyed Johnson checked in on me multiple times. He kept asking me questions. He was a curious man. Trees, headlights, road signs swept by like a juggling man’s oranges. But I couldn’t see them. Johnson probably could. He seemed to want to know what happened to me, but that was personal, I told him, and said I wished he were more professional, that would make me feel more “taken care of.” He just kept asking away. Like a child, I thought. He followed, or rather I followed him into the hospital. He said goodbye to me but I didn’t believe he was real. He was like an apparition from someone else’s childhood. I saw through his eyes the ambulance window as we rode and I wondered how.

I continued to lie on the stretcher as I thought about this. They moved me around like a swing set. Blue-haired, blue-eyed Johnson, where are you but my dreams? I fainted and forgot what happened after because the doctor came in and it was him.

Sunshine motley on the bluegrass. Cabbage-white-butterfly-ridden. I am all a dream. Johnson, where are you? Palm trees, honeyed breeze. I do not know where I am going, but I am going. Ivied alarm clocks. Johnson walks in, but his hair and eye color are different.

The children have climbed on top of the monkey bars on the playground. They are my classmates. I do not want to climb up there with them, because I am scared of heights. Johnson is waiting for me up there. I do not want to abandon my only friend.

“Hey! Get up here!” he yelled.

“I’m too scared,” I minnowed.

“Come on psycho,” teasingly, “just climb, it’s not that hard!”

“I’m coming,” I said begrudgingly.

I liked being teased because it made me feel normal. Lord knows. But still I felt abandoned somehow.

I was all skull-knit flesh in a droopy, rippling gown. I wish I had written more, then. I spent my days doing crosswords, sudoku, whatever little form of escapism I was afforded. Every time a baby was born, a lullaby would play throughout the whole building. The births that punctuated my week. My lull, but one of many.

“Am I going to get in trouble?” I asked.

“No honey, don’t you worry, we are just here to help you. To support you through this tough situation,” a nurse replied, cloyingly.

Why are my conversations always so one-sided? I thought. It’s because I don’t participate in my own life. I saw myself through this portal-like mirage, where on the other side I was an enantiomer of myself. Nothing made sense or had sense. Bland PBJs. Isn’t this place supposed to make me feel sane?

Johnson brought me some pills and water on the daily, a woman now. She took my vitals. They were alright.

“How are you doing, sweetheart?” she inquired.

“Fine,” I replied, deadpan. I hated being asked these questions.

Johnson left to tend to other patients. I tried it all, all sorts of medicines, cognitive behavioral therapy, talking to people, Wii sports, whatever. I am not crazy. I am real.

I went Wii bowling with Johnson once. He struck out multiple times. He was good at repetitive tasks. We smiled at each other but this time he disappeared in a smokelike way.

I went home to my family at the end of the week. They looked at me like I was an alien, while I had always had that view of them. Now we were even, I guess.

Sunflowers beckon me in. I am home where the wood is warm and sweet and the company is brittle, like peanuts. Peanut gallery. Is that all they were?

Remember that the American healthcare system is on your side. Now I don’t mean to be so explicit but you have got to seek the help you need in the system you are provided. I don’t know how everything is just happening to me, or happened. Or what. You have just got to remember that one thing.

But instead I remembered the palm trees. Meadows. I remembered car rides, smiley-facing my breath-embossed passenger window. Bloodshot eyes of my mother. He was always there, watching. My savior, myself.

[diaristic bullshit]

Staring out the stupid window, watching the clouds go by was the world’s greatest pastime. Knock on wood or just knock on the window hard so you can let me out, I thought. But it was too late, I was already here, I had to participate.

Seven Mondays went by in the 36 square foot room. It was like jail except slightly more ornate. I never felt like doing anything, ironically. Come on, I just want to do something.

Crazy guy kept hitting on me. I was 19 and he was 30 something. I had some sympathy for him. After all, I was crazy too.

Sandy was my best friend then. 70-odd-year-old woman who always peed on the floor. Our floor, cause she was my roommate. Always saying some sassy New-York-Sicilian dialogue like “Get outta here!” but said in that very specific way. I can’t explain it any other way.

On my birthday it was movie night and we watched Zoolander. I felt warm and loveable just then, Ben Stiller looking at me like that, surrounded by people who let me pick the movie.

I called my girlfriend every day, we talked a lot, it was probably the most intimate and loving part of our relationship. She made me so weak but in the end, so strong. I have to thank her for that.

My parents visited me through the glass window, against the rules. But they allowed it cause I was just a little kid. During visiting days they sometimes brought gifts from my friends.

Someone was spouting words at the cadence and tone of Eleven’s mom from Stranger Things in that one scene, you know what I’m talking about. It’s exactly what you expect in a place like this.

The social workers were kind and seemed to believe in me. I made them a card at the end of it all. Wow, they really believed in me. I’m out now and I can’t believe that. I hide it so well!

[johnson is gone]

Take your adaptogens and go to sleep or something. There is not that much left to do. They are playing Desafinado outside my window. I can barely hear it through the white noise of the earth. I tried all the real medicines and they didn’t work for me. Made me cry, upset, angry. I pay around 800 dollars a month in rent for a quaint little room. Do you know what it feels like to be all alone?

In my apartment with roommates I sometimes study slash while away time on our dining table, grimacing when they enter my view. It’s not their fault, they are nice people. I write this and I think, not every sentence needs to be wholly unique.

As it turns out, I am actually in a cafe eating a tomato mozzarella sandwich. It is cold. I know you are tired of the lack of images and detail. That is just my life, so drab sprinkled with flashes of unmundanity.

Anyways, I was talking about my medicines. I once tried to kill myself because of Zoloft. Long story but I had a lot going on, as they say. Is Western medicine really so incontrovertible? I persisted for long enough to make it seem so. But I have given up those ways. Reading Tao Lin and watching sunsets changed the way I feel about myself.

They’re calling this autofiction. But what if I don’t want you to know? Nothing interesting is going to happen here. You might as well stop reading now.

I know where I am lying, and you don’t. Haha. Fuck I’m tired of this metacommentaric slop.

Waves ripple like little mountains on Lake Michigan. This is where it happened. The drowning, but I came out alive. Frowning. Haha.

Just kidding about the adaptogens by the way, they put me on another antidepressant and a mood stabilizer and I am “happier” now, if that means feeling blander and theoretically (unphysically) hot to the touch like an ironed out sheet of white printer paper. I can’t explain this any other way, I really can’t, I know as the author you expect me to employ myself here but I just have to say that.

When Ben Lerner said “life’s white noise” damn I really felt that. That’s why I used it at the beginning of this story. Is it a story? Passage.

Time passes like honey. What a conventional image. Time passes like tar. Like glass. Non-ergodically? Sure. I don’t experience like the average person, I don’t think. But that’s just what I think, and I’m sure the reality is that I’m just as ordinary as can be, I’m just not very well-read.

Right now I guess you are getting a feel for my sense of associativity and clustering.

Right now my medicines are making me sleepy even though they are not supposed to. I have nothing profound to say.

When I sleep this time I hope I dream.

[3000]

I’ve observed you since before you were born. You remember many things from your childhood, such as your second time getting your ears pierced at age 2, your yellow and blue sleeping bag you carried with you to daycare at age 3, and the day your brother was born at age 5. I am limited in my lifespan and my words to you.

Let me recount a particular topography of memory:

You were crying in the dark bathroom that day, a common occurrence. It was dark outside and dark inside, all dark such that opening and closing your eyes did nothing. Your mother was beating you, and yelling, telling you that you were worthless, that you had no right to live. You don’t remember now what the fight was about, but I do. I remember everything, even though I can’t supermemorially communicate those things with you. I am here, in your heart, like the blue smoke’s whispers.

There was a curious mixture of love and hate in your family. All that has now transformed into a hot pocket of love, with hatred hidden inside. Your brother loves you, and you him. The thought of him has saved your life many times.

I sound very preachy, I’m sure. This is just the space that I happen to operate in now.

When you were born, I was homeless, and I inhabited your consciousness. I live in your asymmetric smile, furrowed brows, and thumb ticks. Until you were 8 years old, you used to suck your thumb, to nurture me. All your unwitting habits had reasons.

“Do you like chocolate ice cream? Do you want to run away?” you once asked me.

I did. But I couldn’t encourage you to do something that would end up hurting you. We weren’t equipped for such adventures back then, or even now. We must operate within the realm of convention, until our measures taken will identifiably be fulfilling enough, fullproof.

You used to bike to Einstein bagels on the weekends. Sunslit pines and bloodlit Chevrolet eyes on the way. Little pebbles you used to collect, then store behind your backyard, blending in with other pebbles. You’d be the last to touch some of them so delicately.

We’d fly to India together in the same plane seat. I was in all of those homeless people that you felt sympathy for, but couldn’t help. I was in all of those homeless people that you felt sympathy for and could. I was in the turtles at your favorite spot in the whole world. I was in the ocean when the wave kissed the palms of your feet.

I have a whole army rooting for you.

All those simple joys of finding pens alone in random areas of school buildings. All those bloodshot cardinals tittering in each other’s beaks. Patches of holy green grass.

I’m the static in the 988 calls you made. Prismatic light cones through window blind slatspaces. Palm trees in your dreams. Jaundice in the breeze. That texture, that membrane between you and the world.
